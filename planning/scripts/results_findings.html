Findings

  • Inverse dynamics is the strongest regularizer in every environment. TwoRoom 100, Reacher 71 (68 / 74), Push-T 89, Cube 81, in line with the released paper runs (100 / 71.4 / 84.4 / 83.0).
  • Any head that also sees a future state comes back to inverse level. The goal-conditioned policy with G = 2 scores Reacher 71 and Push-T 87 (inverse 71 / 89), TwoRoom 100 for G = 2, 3 and 1,2,3; adding the inverse head to the policy gives Reacher 72 and Push-T 82. Conditioning on zt+G turns the head into an inverse model again.
  • A next-action policy head with at as input only helps where the next action can be read from the state. TwoRoom (scripted data) reaches 90 ± 4.2; Push-T 37 and Cube 69 sit between forward-only (2.6 / 46.8) and inverse; Reacher (SAC data) stays at 11, the random level (13.6).
  • Dropping at from the head input is the largest single gain. Push-T rises to 65.3 ± 3.2 and Cube to 78.0 ± 4.4 (3 seeds each), TwoRoom to 100. Without at the head cannot copy at into at+1, so the encoder has to carry the state that predicts the next action. The signal still comes from the behaviour policy: Reacher stays at 10.
  • The earlier state zt matters. A head that sees only zt+1 is lower in every environment where the policy works: TwoRoom 88, Push-T 57, Cube 66.
  • Neither architecture nor λ closes the gap to inverse. MLP, Mamba and Transformer heads, context up to 16 latents, multi-step targets, and λ over three orders of magnitude all stay below inverse; long contexts hurt on TwoRoom (35–50 at L = 16).

Read with care

  • Most cells are a single seed. Seed spread on TwoRoom is up to ±25 points at small λ, so gaps under about 10 points are not conclusive (TwoRoom one-state 88 vs +at 90).
  • The +at baselines for Push-T and Cube are single seeds; the Cube gain (78.0 ± 4.4 vs 69) is about two standard deviations.
  • Goal-conditioned heads with farther goals (G = 3 and G = 1,2,3) on Reacher and Push-T are still running; their cells fill in when the jobs finish.
